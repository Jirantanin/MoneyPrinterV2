# Cosmic calendar — the human part (≈3.3 Myr ago → 2026) + the future window

Episode frame: the 13.8-billion-year history of the universe compressed into one 365-day year. Big Bang = Jan 1 00:00; now (2026 CE) = Dec 31 24:00.
Scope: human origins → AI, plus the evidence-based future window. (Big Bang → ~1 Myr ago is covered in a separate file.) Researched 2026-10-21. All excerpts are under 15 words and copied from the page or abstract text named. When a publisher page (nature.com, science.org, pnas.org) blocked access, the abstract was read via the Europe PMC REST API record for the same DOI (or Crossref for Wiley). The Source column says so.

**Calendar constants** (computed by script, see bottom): 1 calendar second = 437.6 yr · 1 minute = 26,256 yr · 1 hour = 1.575 Myr · 1 day = 37.81 Myr.
**Dating-zero note:** paper ages in "ka" usually count back from 1950 (or from "present" or 2000). The 50–76-year offset to 2026 is ≤0.17 calendar s, which is negligible for everything older than writing. For CE/BCE dates, "years ago" = 2026 − year (BCE: no year 0).

## Claims

### 1. "When were humans born?" — depends on the definition

**PC01**: The oldest fossil currently attributed to genus *Homo* is the LD 350-1 jaw from Ledi-Geraru, Ethiopia, dated **2.80–2.75 Myr**. A 2025 Nature paper by the same project reports more *Homo* teeth at 2.78 and 2.59 Myr. It also reports a separate *Australopithecus* at 2.63 Myr living alongside them.
- Best value: 2.8 Myr (2.80–2.75). Calendar: **Dec 31 ≈ 22:13–22:15** (≈1 h 45 min before midnight).
- Sources: P01, P02
- Excerpt (P01, abstract): "establishes the presence of Homo at 2.80 to 2.75 Ma"
- Excerpt (P02, abstract): "We report the presence of Homo at 2.78 and 2.59 million years ago"
- Confidence: **verified** (date and attribution as published). The attribution is **debated**, see PC02.
- Safe wording: "ฟอสซิลที่นักวิทยาศาสตร์จัดว่าเป็นสกุล *Homo* เก่าที่สุดเท่าที่พบตอนนี้ คือขากรรไกรอายุราว 2.8 ล้านปี". Don't say "the first human". It is one fragmentary jaw (P04 calls it "fragmented and poorly preserved"), and P02 shows several hominin lineages lived side by side.

**PC02**: Where *Homo* starts is a matter of definition. Wood & Collard (1999) argued that *H. habilis* and *H. rudolfensis* don't belong in *Homo*. On their criteria the genus starts with *H. ergaster*/early African *H. erectus* at ≈1.9 Myr. P04 confirms the debate is still live and that the line between late australopiths and early *Homo* is unclear.
- Values: strict-definition *Homo* ≈1.9 Myr (calendar **≈22:48**). Inclusive definition ≈2.8 Myr (22:13).
- Sources: P03, P04
- Excerpt (P03, abstract): "Homo habilis and Homo rudolfensis, do not belong in the genus"
- Excerpt (P04, abstract): "lack of clear classification criteria that separate our genus from australopiths"
- Confidence: **verified** (that the debate exists; P03 is an older position, not a consensus).
- Safe wording: "ถ้าถามว่า 'มนุษย์' เกิดเมื่อไหร่ คำตอบขึ้นกับว่าเรานับอะไร: สกุล *Homo* ราว 2.8 ล้านปี, สายพันธุ์เรา *Homo sapiens* ราว 3 แสนปี". This is the honest hook for the section.

**PC03**: The Lomekwi 3 stone tools from West Turkana, Kenya, are **3.3 Myr** old. That is about 700,000 years older than the Oldowan and older than any known *Homo* fossil, so the maker is unknown. The context has been challenged: Domínguez-Rodrigo & Alcalá (2016) argued that an ex situ origin (tools not found where they were made) wasn't ruled out.
- Best value: 3.3 Myr; status contested. Calendar: **≈21:54** (≈2 h 06 min before midnight).
- Sources: P05, P06, P07
- Excerpt (P05, abstract): "we propose for it the name 'Lomekwian', which predates the Oldowan by 700,000 years"
- Excerpt (P07, abstract): "predating the genus Homo and emphasizing percussive activities"
- Excerpt (P06, abstract): "the evidence presented in both cases remains disputable"
- Confidence: **verified** that the claim was published and that it is disputed.
- Safe wording: "เครื่องมือหินที่เก่าที่สุดเท่าที่มีรายงาน อายุราว 3.3 ล้านปี เก่ากว่าฟอสซิล *Homo* ที่รู้จัก ยังไม่รู้ว่าใครทำ และนักวิทยาศาสตร์บางกลุ่มยังโต้แย้งอยู่". Don't name a maker species. Don't say "tools prove apes before humans made tools" either. The point is only that toolmaking may be older than our genus.

**PC04**: The Oldowan was the first systematic production of sharp flakes. It appeared at Gona at 2.58–2.55 Myr and at Ledi-Geraru BD1 at 2.61–2.58 Myr. Nyayanga in Kenya is dated **3.032–2.581 Myr**, a bracket rather than a single age. The Nyayanga tools are associated with *Paranthropus* teeth, not with *Homo*.
- Best value: "about 2.6 Myr" for the classic Oldowan; Nyayanga "up to ~3.0 Myr" (bracket). Calendar: 22:21 (2.6 Myr) and 22:05–22:22 (Nyayanga bracket).
- Sources: P07, P08
- Excerpt (P08, abstract): "dated to 3.032 to 2.581 million years ago"
- Excerpt (P08, abstract): "associated with Paranthropus from its onset"
- Confidence: **verified**
- Safe wording: Don't say "Nyayanga is 2.9 Myr" as a point date. Say "between about 3.0 and 2.6 Myr". Don't say "Homo invented the Oldowan". Who made the earliest Oldowan is open.

**PC05**: Fire happened in three stages, and they should not be blurred:
- (a) The earliest secure evidence of burning inside an archaeological site is at Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa, at **≈1.0 Myr**. This is burning in the cave, not proof that people could make fire.
- (b) In Europe, *habitual* fire use shows up only from **≈400–300 kyr**.
- (c) The earliest direct evidence of fire-*making* is at Barnham, UK, at **≈400 kyr**, from iron pyrite fragments used to strike sparks (Nature, published Dec 2025).
- Calendar: 1.0 Myr = **23:22**; 400 kyr = **23:45**.
- Sources: P09, P10, P11
- Excerpt (P09, abstract): "this is the earliest secure evidence for burning in an archaeological context"
- Excerpt (P10, abstract): "from ∼ 300,000 to 400,000 y ago onward, that fire became a significant part"
- Excerpt (P11, abstract): "evidence of fire-making on a 400,000-year-old buried landsurface at Barnham (UK)"
- Confidence: **verified**. Claims for older fire (~1.5–1.6 Myr in East Africa) are contested (P10 calls them "not uncontested").
- Safe wording: "หลักฐานการใช้ไฟที่มั่นใจได้เก่าสุดราว 1 ล้านปี แต่การ 'จุดไฟเองได้' มีหลักฐานชัดราว 4 แสนปี". Don't say "humans discovered fire 1 million years ago". The P11 abstract puts it as fire use developing over a million years.

**PC06**: The earliest fossils assigned to the *Homo sapiens* lineage come from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco: **315 ± 34 kyr** by TL dating of heated flints. A tooth was independently dated to 286 ± 32 kyr by U-series/ESR. They have a modern-like face, but the braincase is more archaic.
- Best value: ~300 kyr (≈350–280 kyr). Calendar: **23:48:00** (range 23:46:42–23:49:18).
- Sources: P12, P13
- Excerpt (P13, abstract): "artefacts and fossils at 315 ± 34 thousand years ago"
- Excerpt (P12, abstract): "more primitive neurocranial and endocranial morphology"
- Confidence: **verified**
- Safe wording: "ฟอสซิลที่เก่าที่สุดที่นักวิทยาศาสตร์จัดอยู่ในสายของ *Homo sapiens* อายุราว 3 แสนปี — หน้าตาคล้ายเรา แต่กะโหลกด้านหลังยังไม่เหมือนเราทั้งหมด". Morocco being the oldest find doesn't make it the place of origin. The authors argue for a pan-African origin.

**PC07**: The minimum age for Omo I (Omo Kibish, Ethiopia) is **233 ± 22 kyr**, from the Shala eruption tephra. Before this, the usual figure was ~195–197 kyr.
- Calendar: **23:51**.
- Source: P14
- Excerpt (P14, abstract): "a new minimum age for the Omo fossils of 233 ± 22 kyr"
- Confidence: **verified**
- Safe wording: "อย่างน้อย 2.3 แสนปี". It is a minimum, not an exact age.

**PC08**: There were earlier *H. sapiens* excursions out of Africa: Misliya (Israel) 177–194 kyr, and Apidima 1 (Greece) >210 kyr, though Apidima's identification is debated. *Homo* in the broad sense left Africa far earlier: Dmanisi ≈1.77–1.85 Myr and Shangchen (China) ≈2.1 Myr.
- Sources: P15, P16, P19
- Excerpt (P15, abstract): "was dated to 177,000 to 194,000 years ago"

### 3. The future ("next year's calendar", Jan 1 00:00 = now; 1 day = 37.8 Myr)

## Story notes

**Most vivid comparisons for a Thai general audience**
1. "*Homo sapiens* has only the last 12 minutes of the year. All written history, from the first clay tablets to this video, is the last 12 seconds."
2. "One whole human life of 80 years is less than a fifth of a second. Your grandparents were born about 0.15–0.17 s before midnight." (Optional Thai tick, after checking the dates: Ayutthaya was founded about 1.5 s before midnight.)
3. "ENIAC (1946) is 0.18 s before midnight, the same length as one life. ChatGPT is 9 milliseconds, shorter than a camera flash." (Don't quote a flash duration; "สั้นกว่ากะพริบตา" is safe, since a blink is ~100–400 ms, and that common figure was not sourced here.)
4. "The oldest picture that tells a story, a pig and human-like figures in Sulawesi near our region, was painted 2 minutes before midnight." It is Southeast Asian, so it lands well with a Thai audience.
5. Future: "If we flip to next year's calendar, the Sun has warmed Earth past what complex life can stand by about late January. The Sun swells into a red giant around mid-May. Everything humans have ever done fits in the 12 seconds before New Year."

**Myths to avoid**
- "Humans evolved from chimpanzees." Say instead: humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor, ~6–7 Myr ago (that date was not sourced here; keep it vague or check it).
- "Language began X years ago." The date is unknown; only a lower bound on capacity has been argued (PC13).
- "The first human was born 2.8 / 300 thousand years ago." Evolution is gradual, and the date depends on the definition (PC02).
- "Humans discovered fire 1 million years ago." That is burning evidence. Clear fire-making is ~400 kyr (PC05).
- "Gutenberg invented printing." He invented metal movable type printing in Europe; East Asian movable type is earlier (PC19).
- "The Web = the Internet." (PC26)
- "Göbekli Tepe = the first temple, built before farming." Function and context are debated (PC15).
- "Earth dies in 1 billion years." Complex life, oceans and oxygen are at stake, not all life; the range is wide (PC35).
- Ages like "≥51,200 years" are *minimum* ages. Don't say "exactly".

**Where the "faster channels for passing on knowledge" thread could overclaim**
- **Milestone selection makes acceleration look inevitable.** We pick the events and we know recent ones in more detail. Lists of "firsts" always crowd toward the present (survivorship and recency bias). Say: "ช่องทางส่งต่อความรู้เร็วขึ้น — นี่คือวันที่จริงของแต่ละช่องทาง" and show the dates. Don't draw a curve or extrapolate it.
- **The steps aren't the same kind of thing.** The early "steps" (stone tools → fire → art) are first-known *evidence* dates, which archaeology keeps pushing back (Lomekwi, the Barnham fire-making find, the 67.8 kyr stencil). The modern steps are recorded invention dates. Some of the apparent shrinking of the gaps comes from the evidence, not from history.
- **Language has no date**, so it can't be placed as a step on the timeline. Show it as a fuzzy band: "อย่างช้าสุด ~135k".
- **Genes vs culture is not a clean handover.** Culture (tool traditions) began long before *H. sapiens* (Lomekwi, Oldowan). Other hominins also passed on skills, e.g. Neanderthal pyrotechnology (P10). Don't imply that only *sapiens* had "outside-gene" transmission.
- **No singularity and no "progress law".** The script can say the gaps between these particular steps went from minutes to seconds to milliseconds. It should **not** say the next step will come in microseconds, or that AI is the last step.
- "Faster channels" ≠ "smarter people". Brains haven't changed meaningfully over the last calendar minute (not sourced here; don't state it as fact, just avoid implying the opposite).

### Claims

**PC38**: Human–chimpanzee lineage split. Fossils and genetics now point to **about 7 Myr, possibly older**. "6–7 Myr" (used in the Story notes above) is at the young end.
- **Fossils:** *Sahelanthropus tchadensis* (Toumaï, Chad) was first put at 6–7 Myr from the associated fauna (P55), then dated directly with ¹⁰Be/⁹Be to **6.8–7.2 Myr** (P56). The Brunet paper itself argued that this implies an earlier split than most molecular studies then gave.
- **Is Toumaï on our side of the split?** Debated. Macchiarelli et al. (2020) argue that its femur came from an individual that was not habitually bipedal. Daver et al. (2022) read the same femur as habitual bipedality plus tree climbing. If Toumaï is a hominin, the split must be older than ~7 Myr. If it isn't, the fossil lower bound is ~6 Myr (P60 mentions eastern African bipeds "as early as 6 million years ago"; Orrorin was not checked separately here).
- **Genetics:** with directly measured mutation rates and wild-ape generation times, Langergraber et al. (2012) date the split to **at least 7–8 Myr**. Moorjani et al. (2016), using CpG transitions, get **~7.9 Myr, range 6.5–9.3 Myr** depending on ancestral population size.
- **Pitfall:** Moorjani's **12.1 Myr** is the *genetic divergence* time. It is not the population split. Don't quote 12 Myr as "when humans and chimps split".
- Calendar (Dec 31): 6 Myr = 20:11 · 6.5 Myr = 19:52 · 6.8–7.2 Myr = **19:41–19:26** · 7 Myr = **19:33** · 8 Myr = 18:55 · 9.3 Myr = 18:06.
